1. What changed, and when

On 12 November 2025 Camex confirmed a restructure to reduce overhead. The COO role was disestablished and Mark Watkins was appointed Project Manager on 17205, acting as Project Director from 28 November. The internal announcement put it plainly:

"Fortunately, we're retaining Mark within the business, bringing his extensive experience to support project delivery which is not an overhead cost."
— Jarrod Cannon, Waikato & BOP Regional Manager, 12 Nov 2025

The handover brief from the outgoing PM covered programme, claim and financial review, notices / RFIs / variations, tomo and risk review, and procurement commitments. Internal Project Manager hours on 17205 stop at the end of November 2025. From 5 January 2026 the same function has been delivered by Maka Projects under a Professional Services Agreement, capped at 20 hrs/week.

This review splits the job at the end of period 08-2026 (November 2025) — the last period run by the previous Project Manager. November was a $101,236 month at 17.21%, and on the raw dates it could be argued into the current period. It is not: Claim #08 for November was submitted to the Engineer by the outgoing PM on 21 November 2025, and the Engineer's queries through to 27 November were addressed to him and the QS. The first claim handled by the incoming Project Director was the December claim, sent for his "review and disposal" on 12 December. The full benefit of November therefore sits on the old baseline.

The two periods are the same length — eight financial periods either side of handover — which makes the delivery comparison direct. The first eight completed $210,607 of revenue a month. The eight since have completed $308,154 a month — 46% more, and $780,378 more revenue across the same elapsed time.

Two ways to read the margin improvement, both defensible:

  • Direct: $451,060 of recognised margin has been added since handover, on $2.47m of income — an 18.30% return on that work against a 6.94% position inherited.
  • Conservative (run-rate): had the job simply continued at the 6.94% it was running at, cumulative margin at July 2026 would be $288,016. It is $567,917. The uplift attributable to the improved margin rate is $279,901 — still 3.5× the fee.
The 6.94% baseline is generous to the previous period. At the end of October 2025 the project's cumulative margin was $13,900 on $1,096,671 — 1.27%. Seven months of work had returned almost nothing. The entire pre-handover margin was earned in one month, November, and it was an accounting event rather than a trading one: November's $101,236 sits on $333,122 of net accrual journal movement, principally a $702,955 revenue accrual raised at month end and a $120,406 WIP reversal. On invoices and certified claims alone the month ran at a deficit. The position unwound the following period, which is why December — the first period under the new Project Director — opens at 5.46%. This review still counts the whole of November against the old baseline.

Had Camex resourced the Project Director role the way it did before November 2025, the same volume of work would have absorbed 2,004 hours and $180,077 instead of 445 hours and $80,100. The project's whole-of-life margin would today read $467,940 — 11.28% rather than $567,917 and 13.68%.

Put another way: roughly $100,000 of the $451,060 margin improvement is the resourcing change alone. The remaining $351,083 is delivery.

Why 2,004 hours is not a 58-hour week

Spread across the eight months actually taken, 2,004 hours would be 57.6 hours a week, which no-one would accept as realistic. It does not spread that way, because the in-house model would not have delivered this work in eight months.

The first eight periods ran at $210,607 of income per month. At that pace the $2,465,231 delivered since handover takes 11.7 months, not 8 — a programme extension of 3.7 months. Across 51 weeks, 2,004 hours is 39.4 hours a week: one full-time Project Manager, which is precisely what the in-house model was.

The same answer arrives from the other direction. Holding the in-house Project Manager at the 35.1 hours a week they actually worked, across those 51 weeks, gives 1,785 hours and $160,399. Two independent methods, the same territory — because once the programme is allowed to take the time the old pace implies, the apparent conflict between "hours scale with volume" and "hours scale with time" disappears.

The extension carries its own cost, and none of it is counted above. Site overhead on this project runs at $27,432 per month. A 3.7-month extension is therefore on the order of $100,000 of additional preliminaries and general — indicative rather than modelled — before any question of liquidated damages, extended subcontractor preliminaries, or the cost of the site staying open through a further winter. The $99,977 saving on the Project Director line is the narrow measure. The programme consequence sits on top of it.

Two further points on the comparison:

  • Both figures are fully burdened and directly comparable. The external rate is higher per hour ($180 against the internal charge-out of $89.85) and the comparison above already carries that in full — the saving comes entirely from hours, not rate.
  • The engagement is capped at 20 hrs/week in the Schedule A Project Brief and every claim is supported by a weekly timesheet. Camex carries no fixed cost when the site is quiet — hours ranged from 43 to 91 per month across the period.

4. Variations — where the margin came from

Variations are the single largest lever on this contract. Payment Claim #16 puts the variation account at $1,118,530 claimed to date — 41.9% of the $2,670,588 original contract price. Of the 49 variations raised across the job, 33 were first recognised from period 09-2026 onward and account for $991,161 — 88.6% of the account.

The five largest variations on the project — VO 32 Building Platform Earthworks ($300,925), VO 33A Lot Digouts ($180,403), VO 30B E/O Ignimbrite ($111,283), VO 24A Carting Rock ($73,582) and VO 34 Lot Import Fill ($48,411) — were all secured and claimed in the current period.

Priced in-house, by the project team

VO 32 through VO 44 — thirteen variations worth $766,718 claimed to date — were scoped, quantified, priced and submitted by the Project Director and Project Engineer, without quantity surveying or survey resource drawn in to build them.

That block is 68.5% of the entire $1,118,530 variation account, and it contains the two largest variations on the job — VO 32 Building Platform Earthworks at $300,925 and VO 33A Lot Digouts at $180,403. Net of the VO 38 dispute adjustment of −$151,244 it stands at $615,474.

It is the clearest available measure of what the role produces: variation value created directly by the Project Director, rather than administered through others.

VO 32 is the case in point. The Building Platform Earthworks variation stood at 0.93 of its priced value — $265,000 — and the Engineer moved to deduct against it. Rather than concede, the Project Director re-measured the works himself in July 2026 using a survey assessment tool built in-house for the purpose. The re-measure identified additional quantity, not less. The Engineer asked for it to be submitted, and accepted it.

A further 0.13 of the variation — $35,925 — was claimed in Claim #16, taking VO 32 to $300,925 against a priced value of $285,021: 1.06 of the priced quantity, and $15,903 above the original price, on a variation that had been facing a deduction. The as-built surface, stockpile and quantity models supporting it are filed in the Claim 16 evidence pack. No survey or quantity surveying resource was drawn on to produce any of it.

That capability is also what sits behind the hours comparison in section 3. Building the measurement capacity in-house is why the role delivers on 18.1 hours per $100,000 of income rather than 81.3 — the work is done once, by the person who then defends it.

5. Where it lands

The 13.68% above is the reported position at 31 July 2026 and it is carried conservatively. Three things sit behind it:

  • The July claim stands. Payment Claim #16 was submitted on 30 July 2026 under the Construction Contracts Act 2002 — $258,098 net, $262,695 including retentions, against $262,698 recognised. Advice is that only $13,000 of it will not be certified. The revenue is substantially secure.
  • The insurance recovery is carried at $60,000. The claim is likely to settle at around $90,000 — $30,000 of upside sitting outside the reported number.
  • A $30,000 provision for defects has been left in. The July adjustments carry $30,000 against rework across the 12-month defects liability period. The project has not been stripped to flatter the closing margin — the position below is struck after carrying that risk, and releases only to the extent the defects period passes without call on it.
